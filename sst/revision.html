02 Full chapter in short

Three parts, twenty-four bullets

If you read nothing else, read this — the entire chapter compressed to its argument, then to ten terminal lines.

Part 1 · Pages 30-35

Gender & Politics

  • Gender division is hierarchical and universal, but built on social expectations — not biology.
  • Sexual division of labour: women run the home (or organise it via helpers); men's outside work is paid and visible.
  • The majority of women do paid work + housework — a double burden, one hour longer daily than men.
  • Feminist movements agitated for the vote, education, careers — and equality in personal life.
  • India's four disadvantages: education gap, unequal pay, child sex ratio 919, violence.
  • Representation is the fix: 1/3 local seats reserved (10 lakh+ women), 33% by the 2023 Act for legislatures.
  • Verdict: when a disadvantaged group politicises its division, it gains.
Part 2 · Pages 36-38

Religion & Communalism

  • Religion in politics is legitimate as long as every religion is treated equally (Gandhi's ethics; minority protection; equitable family laws).
  • Communalism = religion as the principal basis of community and nation — a "fundamentally flawed" belief.
  • Its four forms: everyday prejudices → quest for dominance → religious mobilisation → riots.
  • Extreme form: different religions "cannot live as equal citizens" — dominate or Partition.
  • Constitutional cure — the secular state: no official religion · freedom to profess/practise/propagate (or none) · no discrimination · state may intervene for equality (bans untouchability).
  • A secular Constitution is necessary but not sufficient — fight prejudice daily, mobilisation politically.
  • Census truth: community shares are stable — the "overtake" fear is a mistaken impression.
Part 3 · Pages 38-43

Caste & Politics

  • Caste = hereditary occupational division sanctioned by rituals — special to India, built on exclusion and untouchability.
  • Phule, Gandhi, Ambedkar, Periyar fought it; economic development, urbanisation, literacy, occupational mobility and weaker landlords eroded it.
  • But it persists: marriage within caste, education head-starts, poverty links (rural ST poverty 45.8% vs Hindu upper caste 11.7%).
  • Caste in politics: candidate selection, cabinet composition, caste appeals — universal franchise gave "low" castes bargaining power.
  • Caste ≠ election results: no caste-majority constituency, no caste votes as one bloc, incumbents lose — performance and party loyalty matter.
  • Politics in caste: castes expand, coalition, 'backward'/'forward' blocs — "caste gets politicised".
  • Balance sheet: empowerment of Dalits/OBCs vs distraction from poverty/development and communal-style violence.

03 The journey of a division

Image flowchart

How a social difference travels from birth-identity to constitutional settlement — and where the path forks into empowerment or conflict.

Step 1 · Difference

An identity of birth

Everyone is born into a gender, usually a religion and a caste. A difference alone harms no one — it is just diversity.

Step 2 · Division

Society ranks the difference

Stereotypes and hierarchies turn difference into division: patriarchy ranks men over women, communalism ranks faiths, casteism ranks castes.

Step 3 · Politics

The division enters the political arena

Movements agitate, parties mobilise, voters vote. The division becomes political — this is the chapter's central question: healthy or harmful?

Step 4 · Settlement

Constitution and law respond

The state answers: reservations for women, a secular state against communalism, equality provisions against caste discrimination.

Positive path — empowerment

Feminist movements win rights; Dalits and OBCs gain power-sharing, dignity, access to resources. Division expressed politically delivers justice.

Negative path — conflict

Communalism ends in riots and Partition; caste-only politics distracts from poverty and development, breeding tension and violence.

04 Every date in the chapter

Dates timeline

Ten moments the chapter mentions — half of them data sources, half of them law. MCQs love these.

1947 · Partition
The worst communal riots

India and Pakistan suffer "some of the worst communal riots" at Partition — communalism's most ugly form made real.

1950 · Constitution
A secular state is chosen

The makers of the Constitution, aware of communalism, adopt the secular model — no official religion, freedom of faith, no discrimination, power to intervene for equality. Caste-based discrimination prohibited.

1976
Equal Remuneration Act

Equal wages for equal work becomes law — yet women are still paid less in sports, cinema, factories and fields for identical work.

1996 → 2010
Women's Reservation Bill introduced — again and again

Proposed repeatedly across Lok Sabhyas; cleared by Rajya Sabha in 2010 but never voted in the Lok Sabha.

1998-99
Time-Use Survey (six states)

Women average 7.5 hrs of work daily vs men's 6.5 — her extra hour is unpaid and invisible household labour.

1999-2000
NSSO 55th Round — poverty data

Rural poverty: Scheduled Tribes 45.8%, Scheduled Castes 35.9%, OBC 27% — but Hindu upper castes only 11.7%. Caste and economic status remain strongly linked.

2004-05
NSS estimate of OBC population

The Census doesn't count OBCs; the National Sample Survey puts them at ~41% — making SC+ST+OBC about two-thirds of India.

2011 · Census
Child sex ratio 919 · religion data

Girl children per thousand boys fall to 919 (below 850/800 in some states). Religion: Hindus 79.8%, Muslims 14.2%, Christians 2.3%, Sikhs 1.7%.

2019
Lok Sabha touches 14.36%

Women's share of the Lok Sabha crosses 14% for the first time — still among the world's bottom group; assemblies remain below 5%.

2023
Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam

The Women's Reservation Act passes after decades: 33% reservation in the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly.

05 Laws & constitutional provisions

The acts that fix the divisions

Five legal instruments the chapter names — what each one says, and the gap between letter and reality.

1976 · Parliament

Equal Remuneration Act

Declares equal wages for equal work, regardless of gender. Reality check: across sports, cinema, factories and fields, women still earn less than men for identical work — law on paper ≠ equality in practice.

Gender · pay gap
1950 · Constitution

The Secular State Provisions

No official religion (unlike Sri Lanka/Pakistan/England) · freedom to profess, practise, propagate any religion or none · no religious discrimination · state may intervene within religions to ensure equality — e.g. the ban on untouchability.

Religion · secularism
Panchayati Raj

Local-Body Reservation for Women

Makes it legally binding that one-third of seats in panchayats and municipalities go to women. Result: 10 lakh+ elected women representatives — the world's largest experiment in women's political representation.

Gender · representation
2023 · Parliament

Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam

The Women's Reservation Act, passed after a decades-long pending bill: 33% of seats reserved for women in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly — taking effect after the next census and delimitation.

Gender · 33% reservation
Personal law · ongoing

Family Laws (all religions)

Laws on marriage, divorce, adoption, inheritance — different for followers of different religions. The women's movement argues they discriminate against women in every community and demands equitable reform.

Religion · gender overlap

08 Self-test · hover or tap to flip

Flash cards

Twelve rapid-recall cards — question on the front, exam answer on the back. Perfect for the last ten minutes before the bell.

Definition

What is a feminist?

A woman or a man who believes in equal rights and opportunities for women and men.

Definition

What is patriarchy?

Literally rule by father — a system that values men more and gives them power over women.

Definition

Sexual division of labour?

A system in which all work inside the home is done by the women of the family, or organised by them through domestic helpers.

Why political?

Why is household work a political question?

Politics is about power — and male dominance in the household is a power relation. Wherever there is domination, it belongs in Political Science.

List · 4 items

The four forms of communal politics?

1 Everyday beliefs & stereotypes · 2 quest for dominance (majoritarian / separatist) · 3 political mobilisation on religious lines · 4 communal violence, riots, massacre.

List · 4 items

Four provisions making India secular?

No official religion · freedom to profess, practise, propagate (or none) · no discrimination on religious grounds · state can intervene for equality within communities (bans untouchability).

Quote

"It is not politics that gets caste-ridden…"

"…it is the caste that gets politicised." The relationship runs both ways: caste shapes politics, and politics reshapes caste (coalitions, expansion, 'backward/forward' blocs).

Argument · 4 proofs

Why can't caste alone decide elections?

No constituency has a single-caste majority · no party wins all of a caste's votes · many candidates or none from a voter's caste · incumbents frequently lose — preferences aren't frozen.

Number

Child sex ratio, 2011?

919 girls per thousand boys — sex-selective abortion pushed it below 850 or even 800 in some states.

Number

Women in the 17th Lok Sabha?

14.36% — crossed 14% for the first time in 2019. State assemblies: under 5%. India sits in the world's bottom group.

Act · 2023

What does the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam do?

Reserves 33% of seats for women in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly — after decades of a pending bill.

Core idea

What makes caste division special to India?

Hereditary occupational division existed elsewhere — but only in India was it sanctioned by rituals, with endogamy (marriage within caste) and commensality rules.
